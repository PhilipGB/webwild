# Child usability and engagement playtest protocol

## Target sessions
- 1–2 children aged 7–8, 1–2 children aged 10–12 if available; tests with adults are not a substitute for child comprehension.
- Each session: 20–30 min, observe without solving puzzles for player. Obtain appropriate permission and use offline test saves, not personal data logging.

## Observe rather than lead
1. Can they use WASD + mouse to approach glowing twigs?
2. Do they discover selecting the first and second anchor without spoken instructions?
3. Can they tell why a web failed (distance/silk/missing route)?
4. Do they notice fly trapping and understand the food reward?
5. Do they understand that a new spider is a helper, not a second controllable avatar?
6. Can they recover from storm damage independently?
7. Do they understand saving and returning to game?
8. At age 10–12, do they try optimizing webs/paths spontaneously?

## Record per task
Success without help / success after hint / unable; time-on-task; number of wrong clicks; verbal confusion; camera issues; session desire to continue. No personally identifying data required.

## Gate
If ≥2 independent users are confused by the same basic interaction, prioritise a UI or camera fix before adding content. Do not claim this test occurred if no actual child playtest was conducted; record NOT VERIFIED and finish developer playtest instead.
