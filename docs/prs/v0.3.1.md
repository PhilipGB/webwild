## H5 — Durable exploration, the full map, and integrated acceptance

Finding F9: older explored chunks were evicted after 12,000 entries, so
discoveries could silently become unexplored again.

### What changed (F9)

- `ExplorationRecord` is region-partitioned: 64×64-chunk regions each hold
  one 512-byte bitset (base64 in the manifest). Dense exploration costs
  bytes per region instead of one id string per chunk; storage grows with
  the width of the journey, never with retained chunk allocation.
- `MaxChunks`/first-visit eviction are gone. Whole regions are bounded at
  600 (≈900 km of trail, ≈432 KB of manifest) and only then retire
  oldest-first — beyond any real play session, and documented honestly.
- The manifest carries `ExplorationRegions` additively; pre-H5 saves with
  `ExploredChunks` ids still load into the same record. A manifest may
  carry only one of the two forms; the validator rejects malformed,
  duplicated, oversized or future-version region records.
- The map draws the window's chunks straight from the record, so old
  discoveries are shown without loading their areas.

### What changed (map tooling)

- Pan by dragging, zoom by wheel; the old 84-column window is now the
  starting view, not a hard limit.
- A gold edge arrow points home whenever the Garden landmark is off the
  view, with the live distance.
- Player note markers: click empty map ground to drop a note, click a
  note to remove it. Food markers persist as before; helpers appear as
  dots with their actual destinations and a connecting line.
- The Core note API existed but had no UI; this phase adds the UI and
  keeps the same persistence/validation path.

### Integrated acceptance

One uninterrupted gameplay session now runs the full 43-step journey in
a single game instance (`e2e-*` checks): new garden → M01–M03 → three
helpers → garden bridge → physical walk to Reedwater → R01–R03 → return
home → helpers keep working → wilderness → forest → cliff climb → leap →
river crossing → redundant refusal + guidance → second bridge → food
marking/assignment/off-screen delivery → individual summon, follow and
release → Return Home → save/quit/relaunch/continue → revisit bridge and
regrown food → map history and markers → in-game New Garden with
confirmation → previous garden restored intact.

## H4 — Climbing, the leap and eight environments

Findings F7 and F8: climbing existed only around one authored stem, and
the world was three grassland variants.

### Compatibility decision (world generation versions)

Changing generation must not rewrite the terrain of existing saves, so the
generator is now explicitly versioned. `WorldGenerator.CurrentGeneratorVersion`
is 2; saves carry `GeneratorVersion` in the payload and the manifest
(additive fields, absent → 1, exactly the `HomeRegion` precedent). Loaded
gardens regenerate byte-identical terrain from their saved version —
pinned by `Version1_RegeneratesTheLegacyTerrainExactly` and the untouched
287-test legacy suite; new gardens use the current version. Future
versions are rejected loudly, never guessed.

### What changed (F8 — environments)

- `EnvironmentKind`/`EnvironmentWeights`/`EnvironmentMap` (pure Core):
  eight environments — meadow, forest, woodland, wetland, shore, hills,
  cliff, valley — each with its own height offset, amplitude, palette
  colour, prop vocabulary (dressed from the existing in-repo GLB packs)
  and food distribution. All channel edges are deliberately wide: every
  height contribution scales with the weights, so narrow edges would turn
  boundaries into ramps (found and fixed empirically).
- Cliff faces: a short-wavelength noise channel squeezed through a
  smoothstep inside cliff regions, producing real 55–75° faces between
  flat plateaus, faded quadratically so they stay inside cliff country.
- Rivers demote rock with a continuously fading corridor, so banks are
  always gentle; version-2 water is clamped below the lower bank (hills
  can never flood a shoreline) and above the carved bed (never dry).
- Authored landmark edges force a meadow margin, so hand-made scenes
  still meet familiar grass.
- Worker navigation refuses steep faces (`IsBlockedForWalking`), so
  helpers route around cliffs instead of walking them.
- The mesh, aprons and the map paint from the environment palette;
  `ChunkData` gains the environment grid, folded into the content hash.

### What changed (F7 — climbing and the leap)

- Surface-aware climbing replaces the authored-volume mechanism: real
  collision queries attach the spider to any face steeper than the
  walkable band (normal just under cos 55°) when the player pushes into
  it — generated version-2 terrain (cliffs) or authored surfaces marked
  `climbable`. Walkable slopes, hedges and the ravine caps stay ordinary
  ground, so the ravine bridge story is untouched.
- The authored climb stem is now physical: a `climbable` collision body
  with a standable overlook platform; the old hard-coded exit heights are
  gone. Attaching works from the ground, mid-jump or mid-fall; climbing
  moves up, down and sideways along the face; the top edge mounts with a
  generous probe (no frame-perfect input); jump leaps off with a
  re-attach cooldown.
- The larger jump: holding the jump key while rising sustains the launch
  into the leap (≈2.5 m vs ≈1.0 m hop). A quick tap keeps the small hop;
  jump buffering and coyote time are unchanged.

## H3 — Player-built silk bridges over procedural rivers

Findings F5 and F6: generated rivers had only automatic wooden crossings,
and worker navigation had no shared traversal model for player-built decks.

### Compatibility decision (existing generated crossings)

The seed-stable wooden crossings stay exactly where the generator puts
them: they are treated as permanent old landmarks of the world, unchanged
for saved players, so no migration is needed. Player silk bridges are
additive crossings gated by a spacing policy against every existing
crossing; `SpacingPolicy_LeavesGeneratedCrossingsAsLandmarks` pins the
generator output as unchanged by building.

### What changed

- `PlayerRiverBridge` is a colony construction with stable id, full deck
  geometry and logical world coordinates. `RiverBridgePlanner.Plan`
  derives a site live from the river field: bank vicinity, authored-rect
  clearance, grounded banks above the water, an incline budget, then the
  spacing policy.
- Spacing policy (all values in `RiverBridgeTuning`, normalised): a hard
  `MinShoreSpacingMetres` (12 m) shore rule refuses a site beside any
  existing crossing — generated or player-built — with a "use it instead"
  guidance; within `GuidanceSearchMetres` (150 m) the real navigable route
  via the nearest crossing is compared against a direct crossing journey,
  and a detour within `MaxDetourRatio` (1.5×) refuses the new bridge with
  guidance. Raising the ratio in tuning turns the same site into
  guidance, proving the knob is live (unit-tested).
- Building re-plans from the world before charging, spends silk through
  the exactly-once ledger, emits `RiverBridgeBuilt`, and nudges blocked
  helpers to retry. Dismantling refuses while the player or a helper
  stands on the deck, refunds 60%, and explicitly invalidates every
  dependent worker route: journeys and foraging legs re-plan from where
  the helper stands, or wait visibly when no dry route exists.
- One traversal model in `WorkerNavigator`: generated crossings and player
  silk bridges join the same corridor graph with the same dry-route
  contract, deck points follow the sagging walkway exactly where the
  collision lives, and `IsRouteDry` now samples every segment internally
  so a densely pointed deck can never hide a removed crossing.
- Godot: key `3` / "3 · River bridge" action plans a crossing where the
  player stands; accepted sites preview the ghost silk bridge and confirm
  through the same From → To · cost panel as authored bridges. Refusals
  explain themselves, show a "Use nearby bridge" panel, and guidance
  leads along the bank to the crossing's near end with a live distance and
  a beacon; walking there or dismissing clears it. Built decks reuse the
  modular bridge assembler plus one continuous walkway collision surface,
  instantiated only while the owning chunk is loaded so distant decks
  never linger at float precision.
- Persistence: bridges live in the `SaveGameData` payload beside webs,
  validated by `SaveValidator` (≤ 40, finite geometry, bounded spans),
  exported/imported with the id sequence and built counter. Chunk records
  and their schema are untouched; legacy saves load with an empty list.

## H2 — Food markers and physical worker collection

Finding F4: helpers could not gather procedural wild food, and food was
credited at harvest rather than delivery.

### What changed

- Wild-food worker tasks have a persistent lifecycle: travelling to source,
  carrying home, waiting for regrowth, blocked and none. The broad worker
  activity is `Gathering`/`Hauling`; the food phase is authoritative.
- `TryAssignFoodGather(workerId, foodId)` resolves the deterministic source by
  id, accepts a route only when the navigator finds a dry one, and never
  teleports or grants unreachable resources.
- Harvest depletes the same shared source state used by the player. Food is
  carried explicitly and credited only in `DeliverFoodAtHome`.
- Delivery uses a persistent `FoodDeliveryId`; the ledger grant is idempotent,
  and a reload mid-delivery resumes the physical return. The worker task is
  cleared in the same synchronous step as a successful grant.
- Return navigation prefers the reversed outbound route when it is still dry
  (bridge decks allowed) and otherwise searches for a new route. Blocked
  helpers wait and retry visibly.
- The map marks discovered food (`R` near wild food), draws orange food
  markers, and clicking one opens a named-helper assignment panel. The marker
  and its food id persist in the save.
- Summoning a carrying helper preserves the carried amount and delivery id;
  releasing it sends it home to deliver. No food disappears or duplicates.
- HUD/colony rows show gathering, carrying, waiting-for-regrowth and blocked
  states; events announce assignment, collection, delivery and obstruction.

- `dotnet test`: 249 passed, 0 failed (7 new foraging/marker cases).

## H1 — Worker autonomy and individual summoning

Findings F2 and F3: entering a landmark redirected every helper, and Summon
called the whole colony.

### What changed

- Helpers have a durable `HomeRegion`, independent of the active story
  landmark and the player's physical position.
- `EnterLandmark` / `TryTravelTo` now change only the active landmark and
  visited/region state. They never move, reassign or retask a helper.
- `TrySummonWorker(workerId, playerWorld, region)` summons one named spider.
  Journeys remain physical and blocked routes wait and retry.
- Only one helper can be summoned. Summoning another releases the previous
  helper to a physical return journey home.
- A summoned helper follows the player after arrival. Re-pathing is throttled
  and waits for the player to move at least 5 m, so the route is updated
  without restarting every frame.
- `ReleaseWorkerSummon` stops following and sends the helper home physically;
  a helper already at home stands down without snapping.
- Arrived summoned helpers attach to a nearby authored node when one exists,
  so a helper summoned to an outpost can resume local duties.
- The colony panel lists every named spider with its task, region/distance,
  travelling/availability and summoned state, plus Summon or Release.
- `HomeRegion` is persisted; older worker saves fall back to their current
  region during import.
- The runtime probe now verifies task continuity across landmark changes,
  individual summon, no teleport, off-screen progress, keyboard follow,
  release-home routing, second-summon release and save/continue state.

## H0 — Save integrity and baseline hardening

Finding F1: the in-game New Garden action called `StartNew()` directly. Its
next autosave could replace the previous garden without a verified archive.

### What changed

- `WorldSaveService.PrepareNewGarden` saves the live session, then archives it
  as one workflow shared by the title screen and the in-game action.
- `WorldSaveService.ArchiveExisting` stages and verifies the manifest and every
  chunk record before publication. The archive manifest and chunk directory are
  swapped as one rollback-capable transaction; a failure restores the previous
  verified archive and leaves the live garden untouched.
- `WorldSaveStore.StageChunkArchive` copies each live record, verifies the copy,
  and stamps the owning world seed. `InstallStagedChunkArchive` publishes only
  verified staging bytes. `RetireLiveChunkChanges` retires the live directory by
  atomic rename and rolls back if the rename fails.
- `RestoreArchive` restores the archived manifest and chunk records as one
  payload. Chunk records from another world seed are ignored; a single
  unreadable archived record cannot make the previous garden unusable.
- `LoadArchive` stays read-only for inspection and title-screen evidence.
- The in-game New Garden path in `GardenClearing.RestartNewGame` now requires
  confirmation, saves and archives, refuses to start if any stage fails, and
  starts the replacement only after success.
- The archive is never claimed unless the published manifest verifies.
